## Key concepts in plain English

**1. The Hybrid Detection Philosophy (Physics + Data-Driven)**  
Neither pure ML nor pure physics is sufficient for mission-critical EV battery health:
- **Pure Physics (ECM + EKF):** Knows how terminal voltage and state-of-charge *should* behave based on electrochemistry. When voltage unexpectedly sags or internal resistance surges, the EKF innovation residual ($y = V_{\text{meas}} - V_{\text{pred}}$) spikes immediately.
- **Pure Machine Learning (Isolation Forest):** Knows the normal multivariate statistical distribution of cycle features (`voltage_drop_rate`, `temp_max`, `temp_rise`, `internal_resistance_proxy`, `duration_s`). It isolates subtle, multi-dimensional drift that single-threshold heuristics miss.
- **Hybrid Fusion:** If either or both arms flag an anomaly, the platform diagnoses the issue and classifies it:
  - `PHYSICS_RESIDUAL_SURGE` (sudden cell mismatch or contact resistance spike)
  - `MULTIVARIATE_FEATURE_DRIFT` (atypical cycle degradation signature)
  - `CRITICAL_HYBRID_ANOMALY` (both physics and ML detect severe simultaneous failure)

**2. Isolation Forest Outlier Scoring**  
Isolation Forest builds an ensemble of random decision trees. Because anomalous data points are few and have extreme feature values, they are isolated near the root of the trees (short path length). Normal points require many splits to be isolated (long path length). The anomaly decision score reflects this path length.

**3. Thermal Risk Score (0–100 Index)**  
Thermal runaway is the single greatest hazard in lithium-ion battery packs. The `ThermalRiskEngine` generates a calibrated 0–100 score based on four physical risk factors:
1. **Absolute Peak Temperature ($T_{\text{max}}$):** Safe $\le 35^\circ\text{C}$, warning threshold at $45^\circ\text{C}$, critical decomposition risk at $60^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Discharge Temperature Rise ($\Delta T = T_{\text{max}} - T_{\text{min}}$):** High heat generation indicates abnormal cell internal impedance or cooling degradation.
3. **Transient Heating Rate ($dT/dt$):** Rapid temperature acceleration ($> 3^\circ\text{C/min}$) signals internal exothermic reactions.
4. **Internal Resistance Proxy ($I^2R$):** Elevated impedance increases future Joule heating under heavy acceleration or regenerative braking.

**4. Evidence-Grounded Alerts**  
Every alert generated by the system carries audit evidence (e.g. `"WARNING: Peak temperature 50.8°C breached 45.0°C limit; High temperature rise of 16.5°C during discharge"`), ensuring BMS engineers can immediately understand the root cause.

## What could go wrong

1. **Threshold Brittleness on Unmodeled Cell Chemistries:** The nominal ECM parameters represent a specific 18650 cell. When applied to a cell with different chemistry (e.g. LFP instead of NMC), unmodeled OCV curvature creates systematic baseline residuals. Thresholds must be calibrated relative to the baseline training distribution.
2. **False Alarms from Sensor Glitches:** A single noisy temperature reading or bad ADC sample could trigger a false critical alarm. Multi-point rolling filters or requiring persistent anomalies across cycles prevents nuisance tripping.
3. **Contamination Parameter Tuning:** If the Isolation Forest `contamination` parameter is set too high (e.g. 0.20), 20% of healthy training data will be flagged as anomalous. We set `contamination=0.05` to maintain high specificity.

## Viva questions & answers

**Q1: Why use EKF innovation residuals for anomaly detection instead of simple voltage thresholding?**  
*Answer:* Simple voltage thresholding (e.g. $V < 3.0\text{V}$) only detects a fault when the battery is already fully depleted or failing. An EKF dynamically models expected voltage as a function of instantaneous current, polarization voltage, and estimated SOC ($V_{\text{pred}} = \text{OCV}(SOC) + I \cdot R_0 - V_1$). The innovation residual ($V_{\text{meas}} - V_{\text{pred}}$) isolates abnormal voltage drops *at any SOC and any current load*, catching internal short precursors hundreds of seconds before static voltage limits are breached.

**Q2: How does the Isolation Forest algorithm isolate anomalies without labeled training data?**  
*Answer:* Isolation Forest is unsupervised. It recursively partitions the feature space by randomly selecting a feature and a random split value between its min and max. In a multi-dimensional space, anomalous points lie in sparse regions and require very few random splits to become isolated in terminal leaf nodes. Normal inliers are densely clustered and require deeper trees to separate. The average tree path length across the ensemble directly yields the anomaly score.

**Q3: What electro-thermal process occurs when a lithium-ion cell exceeds 60°C?**  
*Answer:* At approximately 60°C to 80°C, the protective Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) layer on the graphite anode begins exothermic decomposition. This exposes the bare lithiated carbon to the flammable organic solvent, releasing heat and gas ($C_2H_4, CO_2$). If the heat dissipation rate is lower than the heat generation rate, the temperature rapidly accelerates toward 120°C–140°C where separator meltdown and catastrophic cathode oxygen release trigger non-stoppable thermal runaway.

## Known limitations

1. **Synthetic Fault Modes:** Validated primarily on synthetic fault profiles (sudden capacity fade, thermal spike, voltage sag). Real field failures like micro-dendrite soft shorts exhibit intermittent high-frequency noise.
2. **Ambient Temperature Coupling:** The current thermal risk score assumes an ambient temperature around 25°C. In extreme winter (-20°C) or desert (45°C) environments, baseline normalization is required.
3. **Single-Cell Isolation:** Detects anomalies on individual cells. Pack-level cross-cell imbalance detection (e.g. voltage spread across parallel strings) will be incorporated in Phase 8/10.
